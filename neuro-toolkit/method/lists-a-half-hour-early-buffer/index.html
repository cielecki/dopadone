Method

Lists + a half-hour-early buffer

Two simple scaffolds for forgetfulness and lateness: make lists as a working-memory crutch, and deliberately arrive about half an hour early — because you know that if you're late, you'll be very late.

This page isn't typically flagged for the selected profile — shown because you opened it directly.

Two concrete, proven scaffolds. First: lists. Not ‘I’ll try to remember’ but write it down — a crutch for the working memory ADHD lacks. Second: a time buffer. Instead of aiming to arrive just-in-time, deliberately turn up about half an hour early. The assumption is realistic: if you’re late, it won’t be by 5 minutes but by a lot — so the buffer absorbs that time-blindness overshoot. It’s easier to live with 30 minutes spare (coffee, a book) than the stress of forever running behind.

What the research says

Scientific grade verified against the literature. No entries = no direct studies (graded from mechanism/experience).

  • Effectiveness of time-related interventions in children with ADHD aged 9–15 years: a randomized controlled study
    RCT · 2018

    RCT (n=38) of a multimodal intervention combining time-processing training and 'time-assistive devices' — including weekly/step-by-step schedules, written reminders, alarms and graphical timers — improved daily time management and time-processing ability vs an educational-only control. Directly supports externalizing scheduling/lists as a compensatory scaffold, though in children and as a package, not the isolated 'arrive 30 min early' tactic.
  • The efficacy of cognitive-behavioral therapy for adults with ADHD (systematic review / meta-analysis)
    meta-analysis · 2025

    Meta-analysis (~20 RCTs) of CBT for adult ADHD, whose core content is exactly these compensatory skills — time management, scheduling/planners, to-do lists, organization — found medium-to-large symptom reductions vs waitlist and small effects vs active controls, plus improved functional impairment and quality of life. Lists + scheduling are validated as components of an effective package, not in isolation.
  • Efficacy of Meta-Cognitive Therapy for Adult ADHD
    RCT · 2010

    RCT (n=88) of metacognitive therapy built around time-management and organizational skills (calendars, task lists, planning) showed significantly greater improvement in inattention symptoms than supportive therapy — supporting the working-memory-offloading rationale behind list-making.

What the grade means

A A — strongest evidence: meta-analyses or RCTs directly confirm it works (or, for diagnostic tools, strong validation of accuracy).
B B — good evidence: a single RCT, or a strong mechanism with supporting studies.
C C — weak / preliminary: a plausible mechanism, but few direct, controlled tests.
D D — no evidence: theory or isolated anecdotes, no studies.
