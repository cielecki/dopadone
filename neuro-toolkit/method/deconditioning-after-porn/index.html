Deconditioning after porn (death grip)

A hard, dry grip when masturbating to porn conditions the body to a stimulus unlike intercourse ('death grip syndrome'). It's reversed with a looser grip and lots of lube — over time the stimulation approximates real sex.

This page isn't typically flagged for the selected profile — shown because you opened it directly.

Men who masturbate to porn with a hard, dry grip use a stimulus physiologically unlike intercourse, so the body acclimatizes and gets conditioned to that specific stimulation to reach orgasm. Deconditioning: deliberately change the technique — a loose grip, lots of lube — to approximate real intercourse; partner work helps too. It can feel frustrating at first, because the body is being weaned off ‘its’ stimulus. An important distinction when assessing the problem: erection difficulty (often psychological — shame, guilt, especially when a partner doesn’t know about the use) is different from difficulty reaching orgasm (conditioning); they call for different interventions.

What the research says

Scientific grade verified against the literature. No entries = no direct studies (graded from mechanism/experience).

  • Delayed Ejaculation: Pathophysiology, Diagnosis, and Treatment
    review · 2018

    Narrative review identifying idiosyncratic masturbatory style (technique not easily duplicated by a partner) plus high-frequency masturbation and fantasy/reality disparity as psychogenic drivers of delayed ejaculation; lists masturbatory retraining as a treatment but states such psychosexual maneuvers 'appear effective in case reports and case series but none have been properly evaluated in large-scale studies' — i.e. no RCTs.
  • Psychosexual therapy for delayed ejaculation based on the Sexual Tipping Point model (Perelman)
    review · 2016

    Clinical/expert model: the primary driver of delayed ejaculation for many men is an idiosyncratic masturbatory style; treatment is masturbatory retraining to transfer/translate effective solo stimulation (e.g. lighter grip, lubricant, mimicking partnered sensation) to partnered sex. Expert-consensus/clinical-experience based, not controlled-trial evidence.
  • Male masturbation device for the treatment of delayed ejaculation: A case report
    study · 2017

    Single case report: a 48-year-old man with delayed ejaculation completed an 8-week masturbation-device retraining protocol with reported ~81% successful intercourse at one-month follow-up. n=1, uncontrolled — illustrative only, not a population-level effect.
  • Sexual Functioning in Individuals With Attention-Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder: A Narrative Review
    review · 2024

    ADHD is associated with roughly double the rate of sexual dysfunction, including delayed ejaculation, plausibly via attention/arousal-regulation difficulties — establishing that the TARGET population is at elevated risk, but says nothing about death-grip deconditioning as a method and tests no such intervention.

What the grade means

A A — strongest evidence: meta-analyses or RCTs directly confirm it works (or, for diagnostic tools, strong validation of accuracy).
B B — good evidence: a single RCT, or a strong mechanism with supporting studies.
C C — weak / preliminary: a plausible mechanism, but few direct, controlled tests.
D D — no evidence: theory or isolated anecdotes, no studies.
