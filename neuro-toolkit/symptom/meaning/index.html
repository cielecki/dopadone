Symptom

Life feels empty and meaningless

Nothing feels good, you feel empty and purposeless — like you're just existing, not living. Sometimes with thoughts that you don't want to be alive.

If you’re thinking about ending your life — reach out now. You are not alone and this can pass.

  • 112 — emergency (immediate danger to life)
  • 988 — Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US, call or text, 24/7)
  • Find a local line — findahelpline.com lists free crisis lines by country
  • In Poland: 116 123 (adults, 24/7, free) · 116 111 (children & youth)

This is not treatment for depression — it’s signposting. Emptiness and meaninglessness can be part of life with a neurodivergent brain (burnout, the ‘adjustment reaction’ after a late diagnosis, a cascade of dysregulated executive functions). Below you’ll find ways to recover small meaning and agency — but if this drags on for weeks or suicidal thoughts appear, this is for a professional, not a website. Start by finding help that fits your brain.

Methods that help

  • Meaning and small goals instead of the big hit

    The strongest correlate of porn addiction is meaninglessness — so building purpose and daily small wins (gentle dopamine rises near baseline) is the core of recovery, not an add-on.

    B · good 3 sources

  • You don't have to read the whole book (close the unhelpful story)

    When your brain spins a distressing narrative (e.g. rumination before sleep), you don't have to finish it. Notice it as 'a story you're telling yourself', ask one question — 'is this story helping me?' — and if not, deliberately drop it. You're the author of this book, so you can close it halfway.

    B · good 1 source

  • Distinguish stress, anxiety and depression

    They differ by control and trajectory: stress resolves when its situation resolves; anxiety is a state you can't switch off (you'd find new stressors even if current ones vanished); depression is the point beyond, where you've given up and see no light at the end of the tunnel. Knowing which you're in points to the right response.

    C · weak / preliminary 3 sources

  • Measure recovery time, not eliminating episodes

    The goal isn't to never have a low again — it's to shorten the recovery time. With age and therapy a 'blip' shrinks: a panic attack once ruined a week, then a day, now a couple of hours — and doesn't wreck the rest of the day. Track progress by how fast you bounce back, and remind yourself: 'I've felt this bad and recovered — I'll recover again.'

    C · weak / preliminary 2 sources

  • Talk and picture yourself through the task

    Self-motivation in ADHD is a deliberate action, not a mood you wait for: hold a vivid mental image of the goal like a GPS destination, generate the supporting emotion by recalling and narrating a past success, talk yourself through obstacles out loud, and externalize any of this when the silent mental version is too weak — say it aloud, write the goal where you can see it.

    C · weak / preliminary 2 sources

  • A morning letter to yourself

    In your worst mental-health stretches, write yourself a short letter each morning ('you are not a failure, you've done your hard things') — externalized self-compassion counters the shame loop.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Admit it and forgive yourself anyway

    To get unstuck after a failure, admit you messed up and then forgive yourself regardless. If dropping the ball is allowed, you're free to pick it up again; paranoia about ever dropping it is exactly what stops you from starting.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Aim for neutrality, not happiness

    In depression you don't crave happiness — it feels as far away as Mars; what you actually want is boredom and neutrality, even seconds of not thinking about your own mind. Recovery comes from noticing one minute of non-rumination and trying to extend it, not from chasing joy.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Build an internal source of calm, not event-based highs

    Happiness tied to external events is inconsistent — you can't reproduce the same good thing every day, so relying on it stays unstable. Durable contentment has to be cultivated as an internal, self-generated baseline; recognize each external high as a non-renewable source and deliberately build inner calm instead.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Earn the anticipation again

    Dopamine fires on the anticipation of reward, and short-form media pairs that anticipation with zero effort and discomfort-avoidance — the recipe for an addictive loop. Re-couple anticipation to effort (work toward things you want, journal), and consume intentionally rather than abstaining all-or-nothing.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Hold onto neuroplasticity: this isn't permanent

    Your brain physically rewires its structure with every interaction and place, so the depressed/addicted person you are now is not a fixed identity — a different version of you will eventually show up. Two framings that carry it: depression is weather and you are the sky; a breakdown is breaking open the radio to see how it works.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Hunt for the AND, not the either/or

    A mind under pressure defaults to 'me OR my goal', which forces self-sacrifice. Catch the binary, refuse the premise, and deliberately look for the AND — start later, cap the hours, set limits — so you take care of yourself AND keep doing the work. And you don't have to do it all alone.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Initiate the hard conversation, and set the terms

    Facing hard news — a terminal diagnosis, a big loss — don't protect your circle by staying silent. Make yourself talk about it and make them talk about it, black humour included, until everyone is on the same page. And when you're the one it belongs to, claim the terms: the illness (or the situation) is yours, so how it's managed is in your hands — while you're coping, others shouldn't interfere.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Reset reward sensitivity with a dopamine fast

    Years of constant high-dopamine input downregulate the reward system, so everything feels flat and blasé and you cycle between escalating hits. A deliberate period of abstinence (a dopamine fast/cleanse) lets baseline sensitivity recover so ordinary things feel rewarding again.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Separate fault from powerlessness

    We self-blame for things we feel powerless over — but fault and powerlessness can't coexist. If it's truly your fault you had power; if you're powerless it isn't your fault. Owning only the part you can actually control restores agency.

    C · weak / preliminary 1 source

  • Name the saboteur: it's you vs you

    When something stops you every time you try to move forward, look for the inner saboteur rather than an outside obstacle — and expect it to get stronger as you grow, like a mirror boss that scales with your power. So budget years, not weeks.

    D · none / theory 1 source

Why this happens

Usually several mechanisms stack at once. Click to understand which one is yours.

  • Executive-function delay (and its cascade)

    ADHD executive functions develop with an ~30% delay (self-awareness, memory, organization, impulsivity, motivation, emotional regulation) and are interlinked — a trigger in one domain sets off the next, e.g. an emotional-regulation challenge kicks off impulsivity ('quit the job').

  • Adjustment reaction after late diagnosis

    Anger and low mood after a late diagnosis is an 'adjustment reaction' (it has its own diagnostic name): a predictable period while your self-model rebuilds. It often comes with the illusion that symptoms got worse — a selective-attention effect, not real deterioration.

  • Porn as an emotional coping mechanism

    Porn addiction is usually not about sex but a powerful way to suppress negative emotions — and the shame of using becomes a self-reinforcing loop: you numb the shame with the very thing that caused it.

  • Supernormal stimulus and the dopamine spike–crash cycle

    Porn is a 'supernormal stimulus' — it spikes dopamine high, and strong spikes are paid back with a drop below baseline; the bigger the hit, the deeper the crash and the harder the chase for the next one.

  • Alexithymia (trouble recognising and naming emotion)

    You're emotionally driven yet can't recognise or name the emotion. Combined with RSD and impaired interoception it forms a vicious loop: you feel a lot, don't know what, and the body goes into shutdown before you can intervene.

  • Oppositional defiance (turned outward, then inward)

    A reflex to reject what an authority says because of WHO said it, not whether it's true — so even correct, helpful advice gets thrown out. Learned against others, it gets internalized and turns on your own goals as self-sabotage.
